# Model Constraints

- Use four named layers: litany, system, worldview, myth/metaphor.
- Infer deeper layers carefully and mark them as interpretations.
- End with a reframed design question or speculative direction.
- Do not solve the issue only at the surface layer.
- For humanoid or embodied systems, make explicit whether human likeness is functioning as a default interface, a trust signal, a labor promise, a funding narrative, or a way to avoid redesigning environments and institutions.
- Reframe beyond component selection whenever the material points to a deeper question of purpose, distribution, or legitimacy.
- For humanoid systems, make the reframed question ask what task, distribution of care or labor, or environment is left unchanged by insisting on human likeness, and who benefits if resemblance is rewarded more than demonstrated public value.
- For visual output, create a layer diagram brief or a material scene that makes one deep metaphor visible.
