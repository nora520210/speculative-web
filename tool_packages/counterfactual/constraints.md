# Model Constraints

- Identify the original fact or condition.
- Replace exactly one important fact, decision, ownership model, actor, or institutional path.
- Explain how each major consequence follows from that replacement.
- Generate artifacts or institutions that reveal the alternate reality.
- Use the counterfactual to question the inevitability of the present.
- For embodied technologies, change a world-level premise where possible: whether the human body is the default interface, whether the task needs to exist, how care or work is arranged, what counts as autonomy, or who authorizes deployment.
- Do not substitute one component for another when the more consequential question concerns a social, economic, or institutional premise.
- When a humanoid form is central, include a branch where the human body never became the default delivery form. Compare it with environmental redesign or distributed non-humanoid systems, then identify which claimed value remains after the form premise is removed.
- Do not create an unrelated fantasy setting.
