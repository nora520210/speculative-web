# Physical Fiction

## Source Logic

Physical Fiction turns a speculative world into an object, prototype, prop, exhibit, label, or document that appears to come from that world.

The artifact does not need to work as a real product. It should be concrete enough that viewers can infer who uses it, why it exists, what rules support it, and what tensions it reveals.

## When To Use

- A scenario is too abstract and needs a tangible focal point.
- A project needs an inspectable artifact rather than a complete story.
- The user wants an image prompt or object brief grounded in a speculative world.

## Recommended Outputs

Text+Image is usually strongest: a written artifact brief plus a concrete visual prompt. Text-only output is useful when the next step is manual prototyping.

## Misuse Risks

- Turning the artifact into a normal product concept.
- Overexplaining the world instead of letting the object imply it.
- Making an atmospheric image with no inspectable material details.
